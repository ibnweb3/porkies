Can an AI spot a misleading property ad?

Property ads promise big returns and savings. The UK advertising watchdog has already ruled on many of them. We gave 24 of those claims to an AI and checked: does it agree with the watchdog?

Try it yourself
CLAIM AUDIT · UK PROPERTY ADS

00Try it yourself

Paste a property ad claim and the evidence behind it. The calculator checks the sums, the AI says whether the claim holds up, then an independent panel checks the AI. It takes about a minute. No sign-up.

Public by design. The second-opinion panel's reviews are public, so please do not paste names, addresses, emails or phone numbers. Everything you paste is screened by SERV's prompt-injection guard.

02How it works

For each claim, four simple steps:

  1. A calculator checks the sumsEvery figure in the claim is recalculated by plain code, not by AI.
  2. The AI gives its verdictIs the claim backed up by the evidence? The AI (SERV Reasoning) says yes or no and explains why.
  3. A second opinion checks the AIAn independent panel of reviewers (Reppo) scores the answer: approve, look again, or reject.
  4. A second lookIf the panel is not happy, its comments go back to the AI to think again.

04Check the log

Every step is saved in a log that shows tampering: each entry is locked to the one before it, so changing anything breaks the chain. Press the button and your own browser re-checks every entry. Nothing is sent anywhere.

This shows tampering, it does not prevent it: someone controlling the whole file could rewrite all of it. That is why the log fingerprint is printed here and on the badge.

05What this does not prove

  • Small test. Only 24 claims from 11 rulings, and just 5 were judged fair by the watchdog. Treat every number as a first signal, not proof.
  • Our wording. An AI summarised each ruling, then we rewrote each claim neutrally. The link on each claim goes to the real ruling, which is the authority.
  • Possible head start. The AI may have read these public rulings while it was trained.
  • Knowing the rules. Some claims are flawed because a risk warning is missing, which tests whether the AI knows the advertising rules as much as whether it can reason.
  • Reused code. The tamper-evident log comes from an earlier project of the author, Backstop (MIT licence). Everything else was built during this hackathon.
